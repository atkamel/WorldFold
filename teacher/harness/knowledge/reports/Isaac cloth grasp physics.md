# Simulator setup, not policy, drops cloth

The slipping and the "liquid" cloth are, on the weight of evidence, products of how the LeHome environment simulates cloth, and neither the SO-101 nor the controlling policy is the main cause. The claim is strongly supported but not yet proven for this team's scene. LeHome holds cloth by contact friction alone, on PhysX particle (PBD) cloth, with particle friction 0.5, doubled gravity and a 90 Hz step. The closest sibling environment that grasps by contact uses friction 25 and normal gravity, and most other garment simulators do not rely on friction at all: they pin the cloth to the gripper. The challenge winner says the simulator's simplified physics "occasionally produces a failed grasp or a garment slipping out of the gripper through no real fault of the policy", and NVIDIA's forum carries the same carry-phase slip report on particle cloth, closed without a fix. Part of the problem is the solver class itself, so settings can reduce slip but not remove it: NVIDIA's own best friction-grasp demo, on a newer solver with friction near 4.5, still loses one grasp in ten. Real cloth physics points the other way: a small garment weighs a fraction of what even bare plastic jaws can hold, so the real SO-101 fails mainly at acquiring the cloth, not at keeping it during a gentle carry. The team should first run four read-only checks (cloth mass, GPU dynamics flag, jaw collider, applied gains), then test gravity 1, friction 5 to 25 and a 240 to 360 Hz step, and re-evaluate the teacher `lehome_sim` after each change, because it was trained under the original physics. Several inputs to this report came from search snippets or summarisers and are listed in the final section.

## NVIDIA has deprecated particle cloth and demonstrates cloth grasps only on Newton

### Particle cloth is deprecated, and its replacements are not ready for garment grasping

**PhysX particle cloth, the model LeHome uses, is deprecated.** The PhysX SDK documentation says "Particle Cloth has been deprecated. Please use PxFEMCloth instead" ([PhysX 5.4.1 Particle System](https://nvidia-omniverse.github.io/PhysX/physx/5.4.1/docs/ParticleSystem.html)). The current Omni Physics migration guide goes further and says the feature, its integration and its schemas "have been removed", replaced by FEM "surface deformables" ([Deformable Migration Guide](https://docs.omniverse.nvidia.com/kit/docs/omni_physics/latest/dev_guide/deformables/deformable_migration.html)). It still runs in Isaac Sim 5.1, which is why LeHome works; the version in which the schemas physically disappear is not stated in any page the notes reached.

Isaac Lab never officially supported particle cloth. An NVIDIA maintainer explained in 2024 that the original Orbit paper used it, but "this was quite difficult to tune for stable simulation", so the team waited for FEM cloth ([IsaacLab discussion #587](https://github.com/isaac-sim/IsaacLab/discussions/587)). An NVIDIA physics engineer described the solver the same way: "the PBD particle solver we use for the cloth is not accurate in that respect. More particles lead to worse convergence", with the remedy being "higher iteration counts and smaller time step sizes" ([forum: Cloth simulation parameters](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/cloth-simulation-parameters/255130)).

The official replacement, surface deformables, is a beta feature in Isaac Sim 5.0 and 5.1. Its documented limits matter for grasping: triangle meshes only, one collider per body, dynamic friction only ("static friction is unsupported"), and no stretch or shear stiffness attributes ([Omni Physics Deformable Bodies](https://docs.omniverse.nvidia.com/kit/docs/omni_physics/latest/dev_guide/deformables/deformable_bodies.html)). A user on 5.1 found a thin surface deformable that did not collide with rigid bodies, and a respondent said rigid-to-surface-deformable collision "is NOT fully supported" ([forum thread 359023](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/isaac-sim-5-1-ubuntu-thin-sheet-deformable-beta-deprecated-behaves-like-rigid-little-no-visible-deformation/359023)). That reply also contains a statement that conflicts with the docs, so it counts as one user's experience and not as a specification.

NVIDIA's active investment is Newton, a GPU engine built on Warp, whose VBD solver handles "thin deformables such as clothing" ([NVIDIA technical blog](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/train-a-quadruped-locomotion-policy-and-simulate-cloth-manipulation-with-nvidia-isaac-lab-and-newton/)). Isaac Lab exposes Newton deformables with rigid-deformable coupling only from v3.0.0-beta2, which targets Isaac Sim 6.0 ([IsaacLab v3.0.0-beta2](https://github.com/isaac-sim/IsaacLab/releases/tag/v3.0.0-beta2)). No NVIDIA document says "use Newton for garments"; that recommendation is inferred from where NVIDIA publishes its examples.

### NVIDIA's own demos use friction three to nine times LeHome's, at 600 Hz

The notes found no NVIDIA-authored demo of a gripper friction-grasping PhysX particle cloth. Every NVIDIA robot-cloth example found uses Newton VBD.

| Example | Solver | Robot-cloth friction | Physics rate | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Newton `cloth_franka` ([source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/newton-physics/newton/main/newton/examples/cloth/example_cloth_franka.py)) | VBD | 1.5 | 600 Hz (60 fps, 10 substeps) | Friction grasp, no pinning; about 30 FPS on an RTX 4090 |
| Isaac Lab tablecloth task ([PR #7185](https://github.com/isaac-sim/IsaacLab/pull/7185)) | VBD | about 4.47 effective | not recorded | 9 of 10 trials kept both grip points |
| Newton Franka lift ([PR #3920](https://github.com/newton-physics/newton/pull/3920)) | VBD | 1000 | not recorded | Unmerged; friction chosen "so the fixed-waypoint gripper retains the vertical patch" |
| LeHome ([YAML](https://github.com/lehome-official/lehome-challenge/blob/a805ad2f7ab52a4583066fc4ee5180459a7f9d15/source/lehome/lehome/tasks/bedroom/config_file/particle_garment_cfg.yaml)) | PBD particle | 0.5 | 90 Hz | The team's slip |

The `cloth_franka` numbers were extracted by a summarising tool and need a re-read of the file before anyone quotes them. The pattern is still clear: **NVIDIA treats friction as a tuning knob well above physical values, steps six to seven times faster than LeHome, and still reports a lost grasp.** Friction-only cloth grasping is fragile even in NVIDIA's best stack.

### The team's slip is a public, unresolved NVIDIA forum report

A user on Isaac Sim 4.5 reported that after grasping and moving, "the clothing gradually slips out of the robotic gripper and falls" despite high friction. The NVIDIA moderator advised convex-hull or bounding-box finger colliders, smaller joint drive gains and a higher physics frequency; the thread closed with no resolution ([forum thread 333193](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/isaac-sim-4-5-has-major-issues-with-collisions-between-particle-cloth-and-the-franka-robot/333193)). In the companion thread NVIDIA staff called the scenario "particularly difficult since the cloth materials are thin" and gave numbers: damping 10 and stiffness 50 on joints, stiffness 100 on the gripper, and 240 or 360 time steps per second ([forum thread 332704](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/some-critical-issues-in-isaac-4-5-particle-cloth-that-prevent-realistic-cloth-simulation/332704)). A 2023 thread on the same slip advised a single-surface mesh with no thickness, because a closed mesh behaves "similar to a bag" whose self-collision breaks the grasp ([forum thread 250822](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/pbd-deformable-cloth-manipulation/250822)). No thread ends with a confirmed fix.

## Most garment simulators pin the cloth; LeHome is the outlier that relies on friction 0.5

### LeHome grasps by contact alone, with weaker settings than its nearest relative

A code search of the LeHome repository found no attachment, fixed-joint or surface-gripper logic for cloth; grasping is whatever particle-rigid contact produces ([lehome-challenge repo](https://github.com/lehome-official/lehome-challenge/tree/a805ad2f7ab52a4583066fc4ee5180459a7f9d15)). The organisers' environment paper does not discuss gripper-cloth contact at all ([arXiv 2604.22363](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.22363)).

GarmentLab, the base of most Isaac Sim garment work, does not make the gripper hold anything. It spawns an invisible 3 cm cube of mass 1000 kg with gravity disabled and binds it to the cloth with `PhysxPhysicsAttachment` ([GarmentLab Control.py](https://github.com/GarmentLab/GarmentLab/blob/6ee0620fddb8083d5eab554fa343cdc966c61d5f/Env/env/Control.py)). DexGarmentLab removed the blocks for particle garments because PBD's "loosely connected particles often allow grippers to penetrate the garment without achieving effective lifting", and replaced them with tuned friction and adhesion ([arXiv 2505.11032](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.11032)). It still uses attachment blocks for its FEM glove and hat tasks ([Attachment_Block.py](https://github.com/wayrise/DexGarmentLab/blob/e4e298e696bae5d866ded3b31e0ae27becea5376/Env_Config/Utils_Project/Attachment_Block.py)).

The three configurations, all read from code, sit side by side below.

| Parameter | LeHome | GarmentLab default | DexGarmentLab default |
|---|---|---|---|
| Grasp mechanism | contact only | attachment cube | contact for particle garments |
| Particle friction | **0.5** | 0.3 | **25.0** |
| Adhesion / offset scale | 0.1 / 0.0 | not set | 0.1 / 0.0 |
| particle_friction_scale | 0.6 | not set | 0.5 |
| particle_adhesion_scale | 0.2 | not set | 0.5 |
| Stretch / bend / shear stiffness | 1e8 / 5000 / 5000 | 1e4 / 100 / 100 | 1e12 / 100 / 100 |
| Spring damping | 10 | 0.2 | 10 |
| Particle solver iterations | 32 | 16 | 16 |
| gravity_scale | **2** | not set | 1.0 |
| contact / rest offset | 0.008 / 0.004 | autocomputed | 0.010 / 0.0075 |
| particle_mass | 1e-2 | not recorded | 1e-2 |

Sources: [LeHome YAML](https://github.com/lehome-official/lehome-challenge/blob/a805ad2f7ab52a4583066fc4ee5180459a7f9d15/source/lehome/lehome/tasks/bedroom/config_file/particle_garment_cfg.yaml), [GarmentLab GarmentConfig.py](https://github.com/GarmentLab/GarmentLab/blob/6ee0620fddb8083d5eab554fa343cdc966c61d5f/Env/Config/GarmentConfig.py), [DexGarmentLab Particle_Garment.py](https://github.com/wayrise/DexGarmentLab/blob/e4e298e696bae5d866ded3b31e0ae27becea5376/Env_Config/Garment/Particle_Garment.py).

GarmentLab's low friction is harmless because the cube carries the cloth. **LeHome combines GarmentLab-level friction with no attachment, which no other environment in the notes does.** LeHome's YAML shares its field set and several values with DexGarmentLab's, so it looks derived from that lineage with friction cut from 25 to 0.5 and bend and shear raised fifty-fold; that lineage is an inference from matching values, and neither team states it.

One reconciliation is needed here. The physics note treats DexGarmentLab's set as a near drop-in fix. The garment note points out that those values were validated with multi-finger hands that wrap cloth, and that the DexGarmentLab authors themselves report two-finger grippers "frequently fail to achieve a stable grasp, with garments either slipping during manipulation or not being grasped at all" on thin flat regions ([arXiv 2505.11032, App. C](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.11032)). Friction 25 is therefore the right first experiment, with no evidence yet that it makes parallel jaws hold.

### Outside Isaac Sim, attachment is the norm and friction grasps slip during transport

SoftGym states its reason plainly: "We aim to decouple the challenges in learning low-level grasping skills from high-level planning", so an activated picker attaches the nearest particle ([SoftGym, arXiv 2011.07215](https://arxiv.org/html/2011.07215)). Its code sets the picked particle's inverse mass to zero ([softgym action_space.py](https://github.com/Xingyu-Lin/softgym/blob/master/softgym/action_space/action_space.py)). FoldNet++ on Style3D FEM attaches vertices within a distance of the end-effector ([arXiv 2609.12433](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.12433)), and a 2026 simulator-agnostic gripper interface deliberately avoids "contact and friction considerations between the gripper and the cloth in favor of simple positional constraints" ([arXiv 2609.29340](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.29340)).

Users who try friction instead report the team's exact symptom. In MuJoCo, "when moving the franka to a new position with the cloth 'grasped' the cloth slips from the fingers", while a rigid box grasps fine ([mujoco #1480](https://github.com/google-deepmind/mujoco/issues/1480)). In Genesis, a user wrote that this "is the exact same problem I have been grapling with in all simulators I have tried to date", and the maintainer's answer was an attachment API ([genesis-world #199](https://github.com/Genesis-Embodied-AI/genesis-world/issues/199)). The simulators that do model frictional cloth grasping use implicit FEM shells with a real stick regime, such as ThinShellLab's IPC-style contact, which criticises attachment benchmarks because they "abstract away the frictional contact in thin-shell material grasping" ([ThinShellLab, arXiv 2404.00451](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.00451)).

### The LeHome winner blames the simulator and did not retune it

Ilia Larchenko, first of 62 online, lists among remaining failures: "Simulation physics. For computational stability the simulator simplifies some of the physics, which is not always perfect; this occasionally produces a failed grasp or a garment slipping out of the gripper through no real fault of the policy" ([arXiv 2606.27163v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.27163v2)). His top online score was 79.63%. A search of his repository found no changes to friction, adhesion, stiffness or effort limits, so he worked around the physics with policy robustness and left the parameters alone ([lehome_solution](https://github.com/IliaLarchenko/lehome_solution)).

The third-place write-up says "many failures came from grasp quality: missed cloth pickup, weak grasp closure, or unstable contact" ([aarvis write-up](https://aarvis.github.io/lehome2026/)). The notes are inconsistent on whether that sentence describes the simulated or the real round, and the quote came through a summariser. The only public physics issue in the LeHome repository concerns cloth floating on CPU and end-effectors clipping through the garment on GPU; the organisers moved such questions to Discord, which the notes could not read ([issue #36](https://github.com/lehome-official/lehome-challenge/issues/36)).

## A real SO-101 has force to spare; its failures come at pickup

### Holding a small garment needs a fraction of the available pinch force

A two-sided pinch lifts fabric when 2·F·μ exceeds the weight, with two extra conditions for picking off a table: the grip friction must beat the cloth-table friction, and the pinch force must exceed the fabric's buckling load ([Xiong et al., arXiv 2301.09688](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.09688)). That paper measured holding forces of **0.5 to 1.3 N** across fabrics with silicone-coated fingers.

Friction coefficients in the notes range from about 0.17 for cotton on patterned polycarbonate to about 0.9 on silicone, but those figures are snippet-level and unverified ([Tribology Letters 2021](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11249-021-01502-1)). The verified qualitative statement is that "a small high-friction surface is enough to strongly hold a fabric" ([Donaire et al. 2020](https://upcommons.upc.edu/bitstreams/e970dda7-474b-40c8-8536-bc20c15b0533/download)).

The gripper servo is strong. An independent bench test measured about 35 kg·cm stall on the 12 V STS3215, with 0.87 degrees of backlash and protection cut-in under sustained load ([Robonine test report](https://robonine.com/testing-of-feetech-sts3215-servomotor-backlash-repeatability-and-torque/)). The note author's estimate, resting on an assumed 8 to 10 cm jaw length and an assumed 10 to 30 g garment, is 29 to 37 N at the tip at stall. Even 5 N on bare plastic at μ 0.2 holds about 2 N, roughly ten times the garment's weight. **On real hardware a gentle carry is not friction-limited.** This is an inference built on unsourced assumptions about jaw length and garment mass, but the margin is large enough to survive both.

### Real failures are acquisition, layer count and grasp depth

This conclusion has to be squared with reports that real two-finger grippers do slip. The reports are consistent once the failure is located. DexGarmentLab's real slips occur on "thin or flat regions" where the jaws cannot get a proper bite. SpeedFolding's grasp failures happen "due to losing the grip during the stretching motion prior to a fling action", a high-tension move ([SpeedFolding, arXiv 2208.10552](https://arxiv.org/html/2208.10552)). Sunil et al. found slip-out occurs "when initial edge position places the grasp too close to gripper fingertips" ([arXiv 2212.05108](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2212.05108)). None of these is a deep, well-seated grasp creeping out under the garment's own weight, which is what the simulation shows.

Picking flat cloth off a rigid table is hard for rigid jaws. The cloth offers nothing to close on, so the jaws either get a thin finger under an edge or drag two contacts together to buckle a ridge ([Borras et al., arXiv 1906.08202](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1906.08202)). Layer count is harder still: with tactile feedback and a side approach, Tirumala et al. reached 7 to 10 of 10 on one layer but only 1 to 7 of 10 on two layers ([arXiv 2207.11196](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2207.11196)). A top-down pinch that catches only the top panel is the expected real outcome.

Real systems modify the contact. FlingBot "used a 2-inch rubber mat as the workspace and added sponge tips to the gripper fingers" ([arXiv 2105.03655](https://arxiv.org/html/2105.03655)). SpeedFolding added printed teeth, a sponge mattress and a tilted approach with one fingertip below the other. ALOHA 2 applies polyurethane grip tape ([arXiv 2405.02292](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.02292)). De Gusseme and wyffels hold a 2.5 cm deep strip of the cloth edge in their folding simulation ([Frontiers 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neurorobotics/articles/10.3389/fnbot.2022.989702/full)).

Two implications follow for the team. First, the simulated SO-101 and the real one fail differently: the simulation loses seated cloth during carries, and the real arm struggles to seat it. Second, the team's finding that a soft stack under the cloth hurts grasping in simulation runs opposite to real practice, where a compliant underlay helps the lower jaw get under the fabric; it is a contact artefact and not a prediction. The one published SO-101 folding result, 86% partial success with SmolVLA on a single easy cloth, comes from a newsletter post and reports no slip statistics ([Vizuara newsletter](https://www.vizuaranewsletter.com/p/teaching-robots-to-fold-clothes-smolvla)).

## PBD friction is an iteration-limited position correction, so held cloth creeps

The mechanism behind both symptoms is well documented for the solver family and only partly documented for PhysX itself. The table separates what a primary source states from what the note authors reasoned.

| Claim | Status | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Classic PBD friction "cannot model static friction", so "particle positions drift" | Verified for PBD in general | [Macklin et al. 2014](https://mmacklin.com/uppfrta_preprint.pdf) |
| Position-level friction is capped by μ times penetration depth per contact, and "friction strength is somewhat dependent on iteration count" | Verified for the Flex model | [Macklin et al. 2014](https://mmacklin.com/uppfrta_preprint.pdf) |
| PhysX particle friction follows that same formula | Unverified | No PhysX source found; the material exposes one friction coefficient and no sleep threshold ([PxPBDMaterial API](https://nvidia-omniverse.github.io/PhysX/physx/5.3.0/_api_build/class_px_p_b_d_material.html)) |
| Static friction is unsupported for PhysX particles | Unverified for particles; verified for surface deformables | Particle claim is snippet-only; the deformables page states it ([Omni Physics Deformable Bodies](https://docs.omniverse.nvidia.com/kit/docs/omni_physics/latest/dev_guide/deformables/deformable_bodies.html)) |
| n substeps of one iteration beat one step of n iterations; error scales with dt squared | Verified | [Macklin et al. 2019](https://mmacklin.com/smallsteps.pdf) |
| 8 iterations at 1/360 s would beat LeHome's 32 at 1/90 s | Inferred | Applies Small Steps to PhysX, which exposes no particle-only substeps |
| A particle rests at the sum of rest offsets from a collider | Verified | [Omni Physics particles guide](https://docs.omniverse.nvidia.com/kit/docs/omni_physics/107.3/dev_guide/particles/particles.html) |
| With rest_offset 0.004 the cloth acts as a sheet about 8 mm thick between jaws | Inferred | Assumes the jaw collider's own rest offset is near zero |
| Two solid particles touch at twice solid_rest_offset | Verified | Same guide |
| Self-collision spacing (5 mm) exceeds mesh spacing (about 4 mm), so neighbours fight springs and the cloth never settles | Inferred | Mesh spacing was not measured; depends on the self-collision filter, whose behaviour is unsourced |
| Stiffness is a linear spring constant in N/m per spring | Verified | [PhysX schema docs](https://docs.omniverse.nvidia.com/kit/docs/omni_physics/107.2/dev_guide/schemas/physxschema.html) |
| "Excessive stiffness can cause numerical instability unless the simulation is configured with a very small timestep"; use 1,000 to 3,000 N/m at 1/120 s | Verified as staff advice | [forum: Cloth Particle setting](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/cloth-particle-setting/350249) |
| Bend and shear 5000 are saturated at 1/90 s, so changing them slightly does nothing; about 100 is where they become tunable | Inferred | Note author's √(k/m)·dt calculation; consistent with GarmentLab and DexGarmentLab both using 100 |
| gravity_scale 2 doubles the load on a pinch and raises drift | Inferred | The parameter is documented only as "gravity scale term"; the consequence is arithmetic |
| Cloth-on-cloth friction is 0.5 × 0.6 = 0.3 | Inferred | The scale's multiplication is documented by snippet and SDK header only |
| The garment weighs 100 to 150 kg because particle_mass 0.01 is per particle | Unverified, high priority | Docstring not fetched; DexGarmentLab uses the same 1e-2 |
| Particles run on GPU even when the team selects CPU | Inferred | "CPU simulation of particles is not supported" ([particles guide](https://docs.omniverse.nvidia.com/kit/docs/omni_physics/107.3/dev_guide/particles/particles.html)); the LeHome scene flag was not read |
| Only the particle material sets cloth-jaw friction | Unverified, sources conflict | See the final section |

The carry-phase slip follows from the first two rows. If friction is a bounded positional correction per iteration, any sustained sideways load produces steady creep. A vertical lift is short; a 10 to 25 cm carry gives the creep time to empty the jaws. More cloth in the jaws means more particles in contact and deeper effective penetration, which matches the team's observation that bunched cloth holds better. Only a few particles sit under a narrow jaw pad at this mesh spacing, so each contact matters.

The "liquid" look has three inferred drivers that act together: doubled gravity, self-collision spacing larger than the mesh edge, and cloth-on-cloth friction near 0.3 that lets folded layers slide. The physics note observes that bend and shear springs at 5000 are numerically stiff and the cloth still flows, which points at contact and self-collision noise and away from spring softness. Material damping is 0.05, and PhysX exposes no particle sleeping, the device the original paper used against drift.

The mass question decides how much of this is fixable. If the garment really weighs over 100 kg under doubled gravity, its hanging portion loads the pinch with a force no setting of a 10-unit-effort gripper can carry. If the solver treats mass differently in particle-rigid contact, the number is cosmetic. The notes found no source on how particle contacts transmit force back to an articulation, so only a measurement settles it.

The NaN under a two-gripper pinch has no documented cause. The inferred mechanism is that stretch stiffness 1e8 turns the cloth between two arms into an inextensible tether, so any disagreement between arms is resolved by ejecting particles. `max_velocity` 5.0 lets a particle travel 5.5 cm in one 1/90 s step, more than ten times the rest offset.

## Next experiments: four free checks, then gravity, friction and step rate

Every physics change below carries the same caveat. **The teacher policy `lehome_sim` was trained under the original settings, so its grasp timing, closure and carry behaviour are fitted to the creeping cloth.** After each change, re-run the teacher's baseline evaluation before interpreting anything, and change one setting at a time. Changes also only count locally: where scoring uses the organisers' parameters, these experiments diagnose the cause or improve data generation, and do not move the score.

The ranking is cheapest first. The first four rows change nothing and cost minutes.

| # | Change | Exact setting | Expected effect | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Read cloth mass | Inspect the cloth prim's total mass and per-particle masses at runtime | Settles whether the garment is 0.2 kg or 100+ kg; decides whether row 5 is essential | None |
| 2 | Read GPU dynamics flag | Inspect the physics scene's GPU dynamics setting under `--device cpu` | Confirms where the particle solver runs; explains the CPU and GPU behaviours in LeHome issue #36 | None |
| 3 | Inspect the SO-101 USD | Jaw collider approximation (SDF, mesh or convex), jaw friction material, drive stiffness units (per degree or per radian), and whether `ImplicitActuatorCfg` gains were applied ([IsaacLab #4320](https://github.com/isaac-sim/IsaacLab/issues/4320)) | Tells you whether rows 10 and 11 are needed | None |
| 4 | Inspect garment mesh | Confirm single-surface mesh where pinched; measure minimum edge length after `scale: 0.4` | Supplies the number row 9 needs; a closed mesh behaves like a bag | None |
| 5 | Normal gravity | `gravity_scale: 2` to `1` | Halves the load on the pinch and the drift force; cloth drapes more slowly | Teacher sees slower-settling cloth; drape shapes change |
| 6 | Higher friction | `friction: 0.5` to 5, then 10, then 25; `particle_friction_scale: 0.5`; `particle_adhesion_scale: 0.5` | Stronger hold; layers stop sliding over each other | Cloth sticks to table and jaws on release; fold dynamics change; unproven for parallel jaws |
| 7 | Jaw material A/B | Change only the jaw's rigid friction material, cloth unchanged | Resolves whether rigid friction is combined at all | None beyond a wasted run |
| 8 | Grasp script, no physics change | Take 2 to 2.5 cm of cloth into the jaws, grasp at a garment edge, command the jaw to fully closed during the hold, keep carries low and slow | More particles clamped, more pinch force; the teacher stays valid | Applies to the scripted oracle only, not to the teacher's own behaviour |
| 9 | Faster step | `dt=1/90` to 1/240 or 1/360, with decimation raised to keep the control rate; `solver_position_iteration_count: 32` to 8 to 16 | Better friction and constraint convergence; the lever NVIDIA staff name | 2.7 to 4 times the cloth-solve cost; less numerical damping, so cloth is livelier |
| 10 | Softer springs and anti-pop limits | `bend_stiffness` and `shear_stiffness: 5000` to 100; `stretch_stiffness: 1e8` to 1e4 to 1e5; material `damping: 0.05` to 0.1 to 0.5; `max_velocity: 5.0` to 1 to 2; set `max_depenetration_velocity` | Fabric-like drape; fewer NaNs in two-arm pinches; less boiling | Large visual change to the cloth; highest risk to teacher behaviour and to the fold success metric |
| 11 | Self-collision spacing | `solid_rest_offset: 0.0025` to at most 0.45 times the minimum edge length (about 0.0018 for 4 mm) | Removes the spring-versus-collision conflict; cloth settles | Thinner layers interpenetrate more easily |
| 12 | Convex jaw colliders | Set finger collider approximation to Convex Hull or Bounding Box; prefer wide flat pads | Cleaner contact normals, more clamped particles, less penetration | Changes jaw geometry slightly; may alter the teacher's grasp points |
| 13 | Gripper drive | Keep stiffness moderate, raise damping, cap closing speed below one rest offset per step (about 0.36 m/s at the tip for 4 mm at 1/90 s) | Less tunnelling and fewer explosions on closure | Slower grasps shift the teacher's timing |
| 14 | Attachment-block fallback | On jaw closure, bind a parked rigid block following the fingertip to the cloth with `PhysxPhysicsAttachment`, as GarmentLab does; delete on open | Removes slip entirely | Different task physics from the evaluator; hides slip and layer-count errors |

Three rows need comment. On row 6, the earlier trial at friction 1.5 was too timid by the standard of every working example in the notes. On row 13, the requested "softer gripper gains" experiment is weaker than it sounds: NVIDIA's advice was to drop to stiffness 50 to 100, and LeHome's SO-101 already runs `stiffness=17.8`, `damping=0.60`, `effort_limit_sim=10` ([lerobot.py](https://github.com/lehome-official/lehome-challenge/blob/a805ad2f7ab52a4583066fc4ee5180459a7f9d15/source/lehome/lehome/assets/robots/lerobot.py)). Drive stiffness is unlikely to be the main problem, unless row 3 shows the gains are applied per degree, which changes the arithmetic by a factor of 57. On row 14, particle cloth could not be attached directly to articulation links when NVIDIA staff last commented ([forum thread 277841](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/particle-cloth-attach-with-franka-robot-failed/277841)), which is why a separate block is needed. Attach only particles that are geometrically between the closed jaws, so that a missed grasp still fails.

The DexGarmentLab offsets (contact 0.010, rest 0.0075) are deliberately left out of the table. They were sized for dexterous fingers, and the physics note argues for smaller offsets with thin jaws, so the two notes pull in opposite directions and neither has test evidence.

### Longer-term options cost a rewrite and leave the evaluated environment

PhysX surface deformables inside Isaac Sim 5.1 require re-authoring every garment, since NVIDIA says the converter does not support particle cloth and "it is therefore recommended to recreate deformable assets", and behaviour "can only be approximated" ([Deformable Migration Guide](https://docs.omniverse.nvidia.com/kit/docs/omni_physics/latest/dev_guide/deformables/deformable_migration.html)). State reading and reset code must move to the new deformable views, on a beta feature with reported rigid-contact failures and no Isaac Lab 2.3 wrapper. FEM deformables elsewhere in Isaac Lab still show unresolved gripper penetration after extensive tuning ([IsaacLab discussion #4005](https://github.com/isaac-sim/IsaacLab/discussions/4005)). The cost is high and the benefit for friction grasping is unproven.

Newton VBD means upgrading to Isaac Sim 6.0 and Isaac Lab 3.0 beta, porting the environment and the SO-101, and rebuilding garments. The reference example runs at about 30 FPS for one scene on an RTX 4090, and an open issue (seen by title only) reports that VBD throughput does not scale with environment count ([newton #4141](https://github.com/newton-physics/newton/issues/4141)). Policies trained there face a physics gap against the particle-cloth evaluator. Neither option suits a team with a deadline; both suit a later project that owns its own evaluation.

## What remains unverified or in conflict

**Pages that could not be opened.** The docs.omniverse.nvidia.com schema class pages and the main particle page returned 403, so parameter descriptions for adhesion, gravity scale, max velocity and the cloth attributes rest on search snippets or the PhysX SDK header. The Isaac Sim 5.0 release notes returned 404 to one researcher and were quoted from a snippet by another; the deprecation itself is independently verified by the PhysX SDK page and the migration guide, so only the release-note wording is in doubt. ResearchGate, SAGE, MDPI, Springer and MakerWorld pages returned 403 or a login wall, and the SO-ARM100 GitHub page timed out. The LeHome Discord, where organisers answered physics questions, was unreadable.

**Values that came only from snippets or summarisers.** The fabric friction coefficients (0.17 on polycarbonate, 0.91 to 0.96 on silicone, about 0.23 fabric-on-fabric) are snippet-level, and one pair (0.949 and 0.953) could not be traced to any paper. STS3215 rated torque comes from vendor listings; only the 35 kg·cm bench figure was read in full. The Newton `cloth_franka` values came through a summariser. The third-place LeHome quotes came through a summariser. NVIDIA forum threads 332704 and 238275 were read through a summariser by one researcher and described as read in full by another; the advice matches across both. Donaire's slip-time table lost its symbols in PDF extraction, so only the direction of the effect is reliable.

**Conflicts between notes, and how this report resolves them.**

- DexGarmentLab's numbers. One note says the paper gives no values and the code was not inspected; two others read the code. Resolved: friction 25, adhesion 0.1 and the rest are confirmed from the repository file.
- Static friction for particles. One note reports "static friction is not supported for deformable bodies and particles" from a snippet; another calls a similar claim unverified. Resolved: verified for surface deformables only. For particles the verified fact is that the material exposes a single friction coefficient.
- Rigid-body friction in cloth contact. The schema text implies only the particle material matters, and a 2022 thread found that raising finger friction did not stop a deformable slipping. An NVIDIA moderator nonetheless advised setting friction on the gripper pads. Unresolved; experiment 7 settles it.
- Forum thread 350249 and the Surface Gripper thread 309363. One note lists both as unread; another read them and cites staff advice of 1,000 to 3,000 N/m at 1/120 s, and that the surface gripper did not support particle cloth. This report uses the note that read them.
- GarmentLab's attach block. One note describes it as a kinematic block from the paper's description; the code shows a dynamic cuboid of 1000 kg with gravity disabled. The code reading wins.
- Garment scale. The LeHome YAML reads `scale: [0.4, 0.4, 0.4]`; one note reasons with 0.45 to 0.65. The YAML value is used here, and the 4 mm mesh spacing that several inferences depend on was never measured.
- GarmentLab's particle contact offset is 0.02 in one note and 0.01 "in demos" in another. Both may be true of different files; it does not affect the recommendations.
- Newton friction. One note records 1.5 and about 4.47; another records 1000. These are three different examples, and the 1000 belongs to an unmerged pull request.
- Real slip. One note concludes real carries are not friction-limited; another quotes papers reporting real two-finger slip. Resolved in the third section: the real failures occur at acquisition, at shallow grasp depth and in high-tension motions.
- The third-place team's grasp failures are attributed to the real round in one place and left unspecified in another. Unresolved.

**Open questions no source answered.** Whether `particle_mass` is per particle or total. Whether the particle solver runs on GPU in the team's "CPU" configuration. The jaw collider type and friction material in the SO-101 USD. The cause of the NaN under a two-gripper pinch, which no NVIDIA source documents. Whether Isaac Sim 5.1 allows particle-cloth attachments directly to articulation links. Whether `PhysxParticleClothAPI` still loads in Isaac Sim 6.0. How `non_particle_collision_enabled=False`, DexGarmentLab's constructor default, is overridden for grasping there. Whether PhysX cloth springs are XPBD-compliant or classic PBD. The semantics of `max_depenetration_velocity` and of the self-collision filter. How the LeHome organisers made their own teleoperated folding work on this setup.

## Conclusion

The notes change the question the team should be asking. "Why does our policy drop the cloth" has a largely external answer: the environment asks a deprecated, iteration-limited solver to do by friction 0.5 what nearly every other simulator does by pinning, and what NVIDIA's own demos do with friction several times higher at six times the step rate. The more useful question is what the simulated grasp is supposed to represent. A real SO-101 with decent pads holds a seated garment easily, so an attachment-style grasp is arguably closer to reality during the carry than a creeping particle pinch, while being less honest about pickup and layer count, which is where real arms fail.

That leaves a choice between two purposes. For matching the organisers' evaluator, leave the physics alone and follow the winner: make the policy robust to slip with deeper edge grasps, low slow carries and recovery behaviour. For generating clean demonstrations or measuring a folding ceiling, fix the physics locally, starting with the mass check and gravity, and accept that the teacher must be re-validated. The four read-only checks are worth doing under either purpose, because the mass and GPU questions could each overturn part of the diagnosis.
