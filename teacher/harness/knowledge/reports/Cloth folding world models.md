# World models fold cloth, rarely garments

Yes, learned world models that fold cloth exist, but the evidence is thin and stops short of garments. The best verified result is **UniClothDiff (CoRL 2025): a diffusion dynamics model plus a sampling planner that folded a real cloth 9/10 times and a real T-shirt 9/10 times**, with only 10 trials per task, about 500K simulated training examples and four H100 GPUs ([UniClothDiff](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.11999v1)). Before it, the strongest results were on small square fabric: VSF-2.0 reached 95% single folds in simulation and 9/10 on a real 5-inch square ([VSF-2.0](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.09754)), and G-DOOM reported 92.5% simulated cloth folds ([G-DOOM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.12149)). The notes contain no published system that plans through a learned model to fold shirts or pants with a success rate on unseen garments, none on SO-100/SO-101 arms, and none built on Isaac Sim garments or MuJoCo flex. The paper you linked, arXiv 2602.16675, is a small DreamerV2 variant that **unfolds** a hanging cloth (74% on a real robot) and does not fold; Generalist's GEN-0/GEN-1 are closed action-emitting policies, not world models. Your target (a planner with no LLM and no RL fine-tuning reaching about 70% on shirts and pants) is therefore unclaimed territory, and it would sit at rank-5 level on the LeHome 2026 simulation leaderboard, where the VLA winner scored 79.63% ([Larchenko 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.27163v2)). The price of that novelty is that there is no recipe to copy end to end: every successful cloth dynamics model in the notes was trained on random or mixed data, not on expert demonstrations alone.

## The linked paper unfolds cloth, and Generalist is a policy

**arXiv 2602.16675** is "Learning to unfold cloth: Scaling up world models to deformable object manipulation" by Rome, James and Ramamoorthy (Edinburgh and Imperial, February 2026). "Scaling up" means taking world-model RL to a harder domain, not a large model. It modifies DreamerV2 in three ways: surface-normal images instead of RGB, a replay buffer pre-loaded with scripted demonstrations, and image augmentation. The task starts with a cloth hanging from one pinned corner; a single Franka arm grasps and pulls it open. The world model is used only at training time to generate imagined rollouts for an actor-critic; at test time the robot runs a reactive policy with no search. It is RL with a reward (normalised cloth area minus a time penalty) computed from privileged simulator vertices, trained for **1M simulator steps per garment type, one agent per type**, in Unity with ObiCloth. Simulation success with the best configuration is 100% on short-sleeve shirts, 84.8% on plane cloth, 72% on long sleeves, 42.4% on unbuttoned shirts and 17.6% on shorts; zero-shot on the real robot it reaches **74% averaged over towel, dishcloth, facecloth and pillowcase (25 trials each)**, with no real garment results. The paper states no GPU, wall-clock, latency or code ([arXiv 2602.16675](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.16675v1)). It is relevant to you as proof that a Dreamer-class model can handle cloth, and as a warning: it needs RL, a privileged reward, and a separate agent per garment.

**Generalist AI's GEN-0 and GEN-1** are proprietary "embodied foundation models": large models that emit actions directly. GEN-0 (November 2025) claims over 270,000 hours of manipulation data and model sizes from 1B to 10B+ parameters; its cloth content is sorting, buttoning and hanging, with no folding number ([GEN-0 blog](https://generalistai.com/blog/nov-04-2025-GEN-0)). GEN-1 (April 2026) claims pretraining on over half a million hours of wearable-device human data, about 1 hour of robot data per task, a 99% average success rate, and T-shirt folding 86 times in a row; it also mentions RL post-training ([GEN-1 blog](https://generalistai.com/blog/gen-1), [The Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/generalist-introduces-gen-1-general-purpose-model-for-physical-ai/)). Neither blog describes predicting futures and choosing actions from them, so by your definition these are VLA-style policies. All figures are vendor claims with no paper, protocol or weights.

The phrase "world model" covers three different things in this literature, and only the first matches your goal. Test-time search over a learned dynamics model is what VSF, VCD, PlaNet-ClothPick, UniClothDiff, DINO-WM and V-JEPA 2-AC do. Policy learning by RL inside the model is the Dreamer line, including 2602.16675. Video prediction as a co-training target for an imitation policy is what DreamZero and GE-Act do: both fold shirts or cloth, but neither searches over candidate futures ([DreamZero](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.15922v1), [Genie Envisioner](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.05635)). The general-purpose planners have no cloth results at all: V-JEPA 2-AC is evaluated on rigid reach, grasp and pick-and-place at about 16 s per action, and DINO-WM's deformables are simulated rope and granular media at 53 s per plan ([V-JEPA 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09985v1), [DINO-WM](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.04983)).

Three patterns matter for your project. First, **latent RSSM planners have only ever flattened or unfolded cloth**. PlaNet failed on SoftGym FoldCloth in 2020, with open-loop predictions that did not capture cloth shape ([SoftGym](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.07215)); PlaNet-ClothPick fixed flattening by restricting pick samples to the cloth mask, because the latent model cannot represent the sharp jump between grasping the cloth and missing it at the contour, but it plans one step ahead and its authors say multi-step horizons fail ([PlaNet-ClothPick](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.01345)). Its garment successor LaGarNet drops from 29/30 in simulation to 17/40 on a real arm and lists folding as future work ([LaGarNet](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.17070)). Your RSSM that finds the fold direction but scores 0/10 on the finish is consistent with this record: no published RSSM in the notes has produced a precise garment fold.

Second, **the fixes that worked for folding precision were about the cost function, the data and stochasticity, not a bigger model**. VSF-2.0 went from 90% in 8.3 actions to 95% in 2.0 actions by adding corner-biased data and replacing pixel distance with a learned cost trained on ground-truth mesh distance ([VSF-2.0](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.09754)). In VCD's folding appendix, the same model gave 3.5 mm error on a one-corner fold with a particle-correspondence cost and 36.9 mm with a depth-IoU cost ([VCD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.10389)). UniClothDiff's 9/10 fell to 2-3/10 when the diffusion dynamics model was swapped for a deterministic transformer, which is direct support for the UniClothDiff idea your project was built to test ([UniClothDiff](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.11999v1)). MEDOR's ablation shows perception was its bottleneck: 0.651 with estimated meshes versus 0.800 with ground-truth meshes and the same learned dynamics ([MEDOR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02881)).

Third, **planning is slow and policies currently win head to head**. Reported planning costs are 12.7 s (VCD on four GPUs), about 100 s (MEDOR) and 420 s for a VSF rerun, against 0.007 s for the model-free FabricFlowNet ([FabricFlowNet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.05623)). In the one real comparison, the RSSM planner flattened towels 9/30 while Diffusion Policy and JA-TN reached 18/30 and 20/30 ([DRAPER](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.15159v1)). Real-robot VLAs fold shirts at 93% and pants at 63% over 30 trials each, and a policy can reach 100% success while staying in the lowest non-zero neatness tier ([RoboFolDeX](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.10243)). A 2026 paper that plans through an analytic simulator instead of a learned model reports 37 ms per planning iteration and argues that this removes learned-model error ([Sim-in-loop](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.24552v1)); a reviewer will ask why you learn the dynamics at all.

## Three setups one student can copy for under 300 dollars

No system in the notes can be copied whole into Isaac Sim 5.1 or MuJoCo flex: the cloth work runs on SoftGym/PyFleX, SAPIEN, PyBullet or Unity, and the notes found no learned cloth dynamics on GarmentLab, DexGarmentLab or flex. What you can copy is the design. Dollar figures below are the note-takers' arithmetic at 2 USD per GPU-hour, not numbers from the papers.

**Setup A: mask-restricted RSSM with mixed data and a geometric cost (PlaNet-ClothPick and LaGarNet, with the VSF-2.0 fixes).** This is the closest to what you already have. State: a 64x64 top-down image (RGB, depth or mask) encoded by an RSSM with deterministic, stochastic and hidden sizes of (200, 30, 200), enlarged to (300, 60, 300) for garments. Action: a 4-number pick-and-place in pixel coordinates, with the pick sampled only on the cloth mask (the DRAPER deployment picks 2 px inside the border). Planner: CEM with 5,000 samples, 100 iterations, top 10% kept, horizon 1. Data: PlaNet-ClothPick used 1.12M transitions mixed as 10% random, 10% corner-biased random, 40% scripted expert, 30% noisy expert and 10% mixed; LaGarNet used 300,000 transitions from a diffusion policy trained on about 50 human demos plus a mask-biased random policy ([PlaNet-ClothPick](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.01345), [LaGarNet](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.17070)). Compute: 100,000 updates on an RTX 3090, wall-clock not reported; the comparable small latent model CFM trained in 1-2 GPU-hours, about 2-4 USD ([CFM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.05436)). What it has shown: flattening only. To get folds, copy VSF-2.0's two changes: bias 30% of picks to corners and score candidates with a cost learned from ground-truth vertex distance. The maintained code path is Agent-Arena, last pushed 27 September 2026 ([Agent-Arena](https://github.com/halid1020/agent-arena-v0)).

**Setup B: dynamics on privileged particle state with a pick-and-place shooting planner (VCD, minus its perception).** State: a subsampled set of cloth particles as a graph; both of your simulators expose vertex positions (MuJoCo's `flexvert_xpos`; Isaac Sim's cloth prim `get_world_positions()`), so you can skip VCD's edge-inference network and MEDOR's mesh reconstruction, the parts that were their bottleneck ([MuJoCo docs](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/modeling.html), [Isaac Sim prims](https://docs.isaacsim.omniverse.nvidia.com/5.0.0/py/source/extensions/isaacsim.core.prims/docs/index.html)). Action: one pick-and-place, pick projected onto the cloth mask. Planner: random shooting over 500 candidates, or CEM for folds, scored by mean particle distance to a goal configuration. Data: **2,000 random pick-and-place actions**, each expanded into about 100 low-level simulator steps. Compute: about 5-6 GPU-days on a 2080 Ti from scratch, roughly 240-290 USD; the released weights and dataset cost nothing but only work in SoftGym ([VCD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.10389), [VCD weights](https://github.com/Xingyu-Lin/VCD/blob/main/pretrained/README.md)). What it has shown: flattening, plus simulated one-step folds of a square at 3.5-13.5 mm error. This is the cheapest route to a precise finish in simulation, and the honest label for it is "privileged state".

**Setup C: a predictor on frozen DINOv2 features with CEM to a goal image (DINO-WM).** State: frozen DINOv2 patch features. Planner: CEM with 100 samples and 10 iterations toward a goal latent, no reward. Data: 1,000 trajectories of 20 random-action steps per environment for rope and granular tasks. Compute: wall-clock not reported; planning takes 53 s ([DINO-WM](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.04983)). It has never been shown on cloth, an open issue reports trouble reproducing even the rope result, and its deformable environment needs the same PyFleX toolchain ([DINO-WM issues](https://github.com/gaoyuezhou/dino_wm/issues)). Treat it as the higher-risk option. UniClothDiff itself is the most relevant design but not copyable as published: no code link in the paper, four H100s, undisclosed training time.

**Do 1,000 expert teleoperation demos suffice? The notes contain no evidence that they do.** Every working cloth dynamics model was trained on random actions (VSF, CFM, VCD) or a deliberate mix of random, noisy-expert and expert data (PlaNet-ClothPick, LaGarNet), and DINO-WM states that it "assumes having access to offline datasets with sufficient state-action coverage" ([DINO-WM](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.04983)). No paper ablates expert-only against mixed data for a cloth planner. The 1,000 LeHome demos are about 270,000 frames, which is 2.5 hours at 30 Hz, and consecutive frames are highly correlated. The practical recipe, inferred from how these datasets were built, is to keep the demos and add cheap simulated coverage: replay demos with action noise, and run random or corner-biased pick-and-place from intermediate demo states. A sampling planner proposes off-distribution actions; a model that has only seen experts has no basis for predicting them. Two further cautions apply. The LeHome demos are 12-dimensional joint actions at 30 Hz, so using a pick-and-place primitive means segmenting them into pick and place events. And the sim-in-loop paper's planner is anchored to a learned policy prior, which suggests using a behaviour-cloned policy from your 1,000 demos to propose candidates and the world model to rank them; that is an inference, not a tested result.

## Garment folding by planning on SO-ARM101 is open

What is unsolved, according to the notes: garment-level folding (shirts, pants) by planning through a learned dynamics model with a reported success rate; any such result on unseen garments; any learned-world-model cloth folding on SO-100/SO-101, ALOHA or Koch arms; any learned cloth dynamics on Isaac Sim garments or MuJoCo flex; and a measured per-action latency for a learned cloth planner. The closest low-cost-arm item is a DreamZero fine-tune on community SO-101 data that reports action error but no folding success ([DreamZero-SO101](https://vizuara-ai-lab.github.io/dreamzero-so101/paper.html)). A 2026 systematic review names the blockers: "small prediction biases accumulate quickly and can mislead planning", and "there is no universally adopted basic metric for folding tasks" ([Gu et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12921407/)).

A defensible novel claim is the combination: **a learned cloth model used for test-time planning (not as an auxiliary head), folding shirts and pants (not a square), on bimanual SO-ARM101 in the LeHome environment, with a stated metric, modest compute and a reported latency**. Each element has prior art alone. State two numbers: LeHome's binary keypoint-distance success, which makes you comparable with a 62-team leaderboard where ranks 2 to 10 scored 63.5-73.5% ([Larchenko 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.27163v2)), and a neatness measure. For neatness in simulation, mean particle distance to the goal fold is the standard: single-step folds score about 3-8 mm and multi-step folds 20-60 mm on small cloths, and the note-takers' rule of thumb is under 10 mm for a clean fold on a 30 cm cloth, with no paper stating a universal threshold ([FabricFlowNet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.05623), [Foldsformer](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.03003)). Normalise by garment size and take the minimum over symmetric goals.

Four claims are not defensible. "The first world model that folds clothes" is false: VSF, G-DOOM and UniClothDiff fold by planning, and DreamZero and GigaBrain-0.5M* fold laundry with world-model components ([GigaBrain-0.5M*](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.12099v1)). "The first cloth folding on SO-ARM101" is false because of LeHome. "World models beat VLAs at folding" has no support; the evidence points the other way, with future-latent conditioning adding only 3.25 points for the LeHome third-place entry ([LeHome 3rd place](https://aarvis.github.io/lehome2026/)). And any real-robot claim from simulation alone is unsafe: LaGarNet lost more than half its success moving to a real arm. If your model uses simulator particle state, say so; a privileged-state planner is not comparable to the camera-only leaderboard entries without that caveat.

## Conclusion

The literature gives you a narrow but real opening. The one verified garment fold by a learned-dynamics planner used exactly the ingredients your project set out to test, a stochastic diffusion dynamics model with a state estimator and a sampling planner, and its ablation says the stochastic model is what separated 9/10 from 2/10. Nobody has repeated it on cheap arms, on pants, on unseen garments, or with a published latency. Your 0/10 finish is the expected failure of an image-latent RSSM on folding, and the published remedies are specific: a geometric cost built on particle correspondence, corner-biased and noisy data on top of the demos, mask-restricted picks, and privileged particle state while you are still in simulation.

The risk is not being scooped by another world model; it is that a tuned VLA already scores 64-80% on the same benchmark and an analytic simulator plans in 37 ms. A result of about 70% on the LeHome criterion with a planner, a neatness number in millimetres, and a per-action latency would be new on method, garment and hardware at once, provided the write-up says plainly which state was privileged and stays in simulation unless a real arm was actually tested.
