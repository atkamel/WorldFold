## The data: what goes where

Per control step (20 Hz) the env produces one **139-D state vector**, three **camera
frames**, and labels. A policy outputs one **12-D action** per step, in chunks of 16.

| stream | shape | contents | on a real robot? |
|---|---|---|---|
| `state` 0-49 | float32 [50] | per arm: 5 joint positions, 5 joint velocities, gripper command, end-effector position (3) + quaternion (4) + velocity (6), grasp flag | yes, except the 2 grasp flags (24, 49) |
| `state` 50-118 | float32 [69] | cloth: 4 corner positions + velocities, 9 sampled vertices, centre of mass, height min/max/mean, corner-to-goal vectors | no (privileged) |
| `state` 119-138 | float32 [20] | task: mean progress, 4 goal keypoints, per-corner progress, time left, expert stage, settle counter | no (privileged) |
| `main` | uint8 [3,128,128] | fixed overhead-oblique camera, fovy 45° | yes |
| `left_wrist_cam`, `right_wrist_cam` | uint8 [3,64,64] | one camera per gripper, fovy 75° | yes |
| action | float32 [12] in [-1, 1] | per arm: 5 joint deltas (× 0.05 rad) + gripper | — |

**Why 139 and not 141:** the base env emits 141. M1.1 drops its 4-dim task one-hot (constant
for one task) and appends the expert's stage and settle counter: 141 − 4 + 2 = 139.
**Proprio** means the 48 sensor-available arm dims (0-49 without the grasp flags).

**Who consumes what:** state policies (BC, DAgger, diffusion, IQL actor) read the full 139 ×
2-step history. The vision policy reads the 3 cameras + the 48 proprio dims (the other 91 are
zeroed). The success detector reads the main camera only. The IQL critic reads 139 × 2 plus
an 8 × 12 macro-action. The scripted expert reads the sim state directly.

## Phase 5b — close the gaps

| milestone | question | result | state |
|---|---|---|---|
| M5b.1 | Does DAgger from the diffusion head beat chunk-MLP DAgger? | round 1: 98.0 / 72.0 / 72.5, +1.74 SE over `dagger_v2/round_3` | ✅ |
| M5b.2 | Do shifted-pose rollouts lift shifted to ≥ 85%? | no: 61-64.5% shifted; the failures are S1 fine-placement stalls, not coverage | ❌ closed |
| M5b.3 | Does distillation bring vision recovery within 15 pp of the teacher? | no: rounds pulled shifted down (92 → 68) and left recovery flat (30-35) | ❌ closed |
| M5b.4 | Does offline RL work with diverse actions? | no: diversity tripled the advantage spread but not the success-vs-failure gap (+0.003); iql_v4 89.0 / 34.0 / 34.5 (replan 8), 69.0 / 18.5 / 25.5 (replan 4) vs 98 / 72 / 72.5 | ❌ closed, **RL dropped** |
| M5b.5 | Do earlier numbers reproduce under deterministic eval? | yes: repeat identical; all re-evals inside the original intervals | ✅ |
| M5b.6 | Does tighter replanning fix fine placement? | partly: privileged 99.5 / 77.0 / 79.5 at replan 4, vision recovery 30 → 41% at replan 2; shifted < 85% | ❌ closed, adopted |

### Findings worth remembering
- **Replanning is a free lever.** Re-planning the 16-step chunk every 4 steps instead of 8
  gives the privileged policy +5 pp shifted and +7 pp recovery without retraining. The vision
  student's recovery rises monotonically to replan 2.
- **Distillation transfers the teacher's weaknesses, not its strengths.** The camera student
  starts *better* than its teacher on shifted poses (92 vs 72%). Each on-policy round pulled it
  toward the teacher's 72%.
- **More shifted data doesn't fix fine placement.** The remaining shifted failures are the
  last centimetre, which the expert closes with a slow feedback re-place a chunk policy
  under-imitates.
- **Offline RL needed action diversity, and got it.** Four policies × two noise levels gave the
  critic 3× the advantage spread (0.054 vs 0.018). The first try collapsed because the actor was
  fed 75% failed-episode actions (a sampling design bug, fixed for iql_v4).
- **Diffusion seed variance is larger than chunk-MLP's.** Three diffusion BC seeds span 77-85% on shifted (8 pp), about the size of the Phase 5b effects. Seed 0, the start of every Phase 5b experiment, is the weakest; plain BC seed 2 reaches the 85% shifted target (recovery 60.5%). Two vision seeds give recovery 41 and 34.5%. From here on, milestones are judged on ≥ 2 seeds.

## Phase 5c — GPU throughput

| milestone | result | state |
|---|---|---|
| M5c.1 profile | physics is ~50% of worker time in every loop without the scripted expert; the expert's label look-ahead is 43% of expert-DAgger worker time (2.4× slower per step than eval); main-process inference 1.7-6.0% | ✅ |
| M5c.2 GPU inference | the 10-step diffusion sampler as one CUDA graph: 301 → 24 ms per batched call (12.7×), bit-identical actions. Share of rollout wall time: 2.7% vision eval, 4.8% DAgger with GPU policy-teacher labels, 1.7% expert DAgger, **6.0% diffusion state eval** (exit < 5%). | ❌ closed narrowly |
| M5c.3 overlap GPU and CPU | the cross-process CPU slot keeps one 10-worker sim pool across concurrent queues; GPU kernel-active 27% across a DAgger run (32% with extra lanes, 22% alone), target > 50% | ❌ closed |
| M5c.4 GPU physics (MuJoCo Warp) | loads the cloth model, but 274 world-steps/s at best vs 576 on one CPU core, and drifts > 1 cm from the CPU trajectory by step 15 | ❌ closed |
| M5c.5 batched GPU rendering | gated on M5c.4 | ❌ closed |

Also moved to the GPU on the way: DAgger labels from a policy teacher run in the main
process on the GPU (the workers only simulate); training batches are gathered from GPU-resident
tensors (lazy `WindowSampler`, frames on the GPU when they fit); training runs in bf16. The
pipeline stays CPU-physics-bound: each control step is 100 cloth substeps (~200 ms of one
core). The sim is capped at 10 of 16 cores, one simulation pool at a time (`IMITATION_CPU_SLOT`).
